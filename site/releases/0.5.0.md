Blizzard through Playport's new **Battle.net** store, sign in and step into Azeroth. **Red

## Highlights

### ⚔️ World of Warcraft, from Battle.net
**Battle.net is now a store in Playport**, next to Steam, GOG and Epic Games, and World of
Warcraft shows in everyone's Library. No Battle.net app and no sign-in in Playport: pick
the game, press Install, and Playport downloads it from Blizzard's own servers.

- **Retail, Classic, Classic Era and Classic Anniversary**, each with a region picker (US,
  Europe, Korea or Taiwan; your phone's region by default) and a language. Playport checks
  the free space before it starts and installs through Downloads, with pause, resume and
  cancel.
- **Always up to date:** every Play checks for a new build first and updates through
  Downloads, then plays. *Check for update* sits on the game's page too.
- **The game signs itself in**, as on a PC. The in-game menu's new **Type text** opens a
  field over the game for your email, password and authenticator code, with paste and
  AutoFill.
- **It plays.** In our testing World of Warcraft reaches its login screen within about
  45 seconds of Play and runs in the world at **55–60 FPS**.
- **Your game, untouched:** Playport runs Blizzard's own Windows client through its
  translator and changes none of the game's files and none of its code in memory. Before
  the first Play it tells you plainly that Blizzard does not support Playport.

![The Library's Battle.net store with World of Warcraft](https://playport.dev/screenshots/library-battlenet.webp)

### 🤠 Red Dead Redemption 2 rides
Press Play and Rockstar's own launcher starts, signs in, hands the game its ticket and gets
out of the way. Social Club runs on Rockstar's own components, so the game stays signed in
and saves as it should. One press on Story and you are in Arthur's cabin, on the frontier,
on a phone.

![Arthur Morgan in a winter coat on a snowy cabin porch, snow-covered pines beyond](https://playport.dev/screenshots/rdr2.webp)

### 🐺 The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Remastered plays
The remastered 4.x build of The Witcher 3 used to stop the moment it started: it needs
AVX instructions the phone could not offer and asks for far more memory space than an
iPhone app gets. Playport now handles both, and Geralt is back in Kaer Morhen, on the
game's Direct3D 12 renderer. The classic 1.32 build still plays as before.

![Geralt walking through a hall of Kaer Morhen past a lit fireplace](https://playport.dev/screenshots/witcher3-remastered.webp)

### 🎮 On-screen controls: play with nothing but the phone
No controller? Playport draws its **on-screen controls** over the game. Connect a controller
and they step aside; disconnect it and they come back (*Settings › Controls*: Automatic,
Always or Never).

- **Three layouts, plus Off:** **Gamepad** (sticks, d-pad, A/B/X/Y, bumpers, triggers, View
  and Menu), **Keyboard & mouse** (a W A S D stick, drag anywhere free to look, key caps
  around your thumb) and **Point & click** (tap to click or use it as a trackpad, with a
  magnifier for small targets, plus right click, hover, scroll, keys and Esc).
- **A three-finger tap** anywhere opens Playport's in-game menu, with any layout. The first
  time the controls appear on their own, a toast says so.
- **The layout editor:** add, move, resize, duplicate and remove controls on a grid with
  snapping guides, undo, reset and switch presets, by touch or with a controller, over the
  paused game or from *Game options › Controls*. Each game keeps its own layout and its own
  opacity.
- **A suggested starting layout:** a game that never asks for a controller in its first
  20 seconds is offered Keyboard & mouse, once.
- **A touch keyboard** over the game, with F keys, arrows and sticky Shift, Ctrl and Alt,
  for consoles, names and the keys a layout lacks.
- **Any controller as a keyboard and mouse:** *Game options › Controls › Controller* maps
  every button, stick, trigger and gyro to a controller input, a key with modifiers, a
  mouse button, mouse movement or the wheel, or a Playport action. Pick **As a controller**,
  **As keyboard & mouse** or **Custom**.
- **Gyro aim:** aim by moving the phone, or a DualSense, DualShock 4 or Switch Pro
  controller, as the mouse or the right stick, per game, with sensitivity, smoothing and a
  test area to try it before you play.

### 📚 A redesigned Library
The stores are now **a column of icons down the Library's left**: All, Steam, GOG, Epic
Games and Battle.net, every one always there (a store you are signed out of is dimmed).
The chosen store's name and game count sit above the grid, and **Installed only** narrows
any of them with one tap. With a controller, press left from the first column to reach the
stores, or step through them from anywhere with LT and RT.

![The Library with All games and the store column](https://playport.dev/screenshots/library-0.5.webp)

### 📊 A performance overlay
A small pill at the top of the game shows the **frame rate, the frame time and your phone's
thermal state**. Turn it on from the in-game menu or *Settings › Graphics*, and it stays on
for every game. It replaces Apple's Metal HUD, which cost CPU time on every frame.

## Also new
- **Vulkan is every game's graphics backend.** Direct3D 9, 10, 11 and 12 now run on Vulkan
  over Metal by default. DXMT stays one choice away in *Settings › Graphics › Direct3D* or
  on the game's page.
- **Quicker launches:** shaders are compiled faster for Metal on iOS 27, and what the
  graphics backends and the code translator have done is kept on disk, so a second launch
  skips the first one's work. *Settings › Storage › Shader cache* shows the space and
  clears it.
- **OpenGL games run**, through Mesa's Zink on Vulkan, in 64-bit and 32-bit games alike.
- **More room for big games:** each Play's memory is planned from your phone's memory and
  the game's needs, freed memory is reused, and *Settings › Graphics › Donor memory*
  (Auto by default) lets a game use more than the app alone could hold.
- **Steam for 32-bit games too:** Playport's own Steam client now serves 32-bit games the
  same sign-in, ownership check and online tickets as 64-bit ones. **Repair** fetches
  missing Steamworks Shared files.
- **Games get what they need, from Play:** an *Installing dependencies* step runs a game's
  own prerequisite installer once, fetches Steam's or GOG's redistributables, and carries
  Wine Mono for .NET games.
- **Publisher launchers:** Rockstar's launcher runs for the games that need it, and a
  game's own launcher and dialog windows show instead of an empty screen.
- **Type text** in the in-game menu sends typing, paste and AutoFill to the game's focused
  field, with a password mode.
- **Graphics choices are cleaner:** *Aspect ratio* and *Resolution* are separate, and a
  game can carry its own compatibility defaults (Super Meat Boy's 16:9, Assassin's Creed's
  Keyboard & mouse layout).
- **Screenshot** works over Vulkan games, and games that change display mode keep showing.
- **Settings scrolls** on a landscape phone; *Storage* lists Steamworks Shared, the shader
  cache and the translator's cache with their sizes.
- **Newer components:** Wine 11.20 with Valve's latest Proton Wine changes, and current
  FEX (the translator that runs x86 code on the phone), DXMT, DXVK, vkd3d-proton and Mesa.

## Good to know
- **World of Warcraft needs your Battle.net account** with an active subscription; the
  game's own login checks it. Blizzard does not support Playport, and an action on your
  account is possible: Playport asks you to accept that once before the first Play.
- **World of Warcraft is big:** retail needs about 132 GB of free space, Classic about
  80 GB and Classic Era about 5 GB. Our testing covered retail. Busy cities and raids may
  run slower than the open world.
- **Red Dead Redemption 2 and The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Remastered push the phone to its
  limits.** Both run close to the app's memory limit and need the increased memory limit
  from your sideloader. Both heat the phone, and their frame rate drops as it warms;
  expect long loads and hitches. Red Dead Redemption 2 installs about 120 GB, and we test
  Story mode only, never Red Dead Online. In bright light some highlights look wrong (a
  white beard brim, a red ear).
- **Vulkan is the default now.** If a game looks or behaves worse than on 0.4.0, switch it
  to **DXMT** on its page (*Game options › Graphics › Direct3D*) and tell us which game.
- **On-screen controls:** without a controller, the three-finger tap is the only way into
  the in-game menu. In some games' menus a Point & click tap moves the highlight without
  opening the item (Portal 2's). A game may show controller prompts while the layout is
  Off. Gyro aim drives the game's own stick or mouse, so a game's own aim settings still
  apply.
- **Big games keep needing memory,** and a game that wants more than the phone can hold
  still ends.
- **Tested on one phone** (iOS 27.2). If a game misbehaves, *Report a problem* on its page
  shares one zip with its logs.
- Your games, saves and logins are untouched by this update; install over 0.4.0.

## Install

Download `Playport-0.5.0.ipa` and sideload it with your own Apple ID, keeping the
increased memory limit and the JIT helper extension. If you have an earlier version,
install over it: your games, saves and logins stay. Bring your own games from Steam, GOG,
Epic Games or Files, or install World of Warcraft from Battle.net.

The complete Corresponding Source of this exact build, the notices and the rebuild
instructions are attached as `Playport-0.5.0-source.tar`, `NOTICES.tar` and
`INSTALL-REBUILD.tar`. `SHA256SUMS` covers every file.
