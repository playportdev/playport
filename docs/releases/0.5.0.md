# Playport 0.5.0: play without a controller, Vulkan for every game, and Red Dead Redemption 2

**No controller? Play anyway.** Playport now draws an on-screen gamepad over the
game, aims with your phone's or your controller's gyro, and turns a physical
controller into a keyboard and mouse. **Vulkan is now every game's graphics
backend**, OpenGL games run, big games get more memory — and **Red Dead Redemption 2
and The Witcher 3's 4.x build play.**

## Highlights

### 🎮 On-screen controls, for playing without a controller
With no controller connected, a game with the **Gamepad** layout shows Playport's
controls over the game as it starts: **A/B/X/Y**, **LB/RB** and **LT/RT**, **View** and
**Menu**, a **left stick** zone with a d-pad above it when you turn it on, and a
**right stick** zone you can place below or above A/B/X/Y.

- **A three-finger tap** anywhere opens Playport's in-game menu (Resume, Controls,
  Screenshot, Performance overlay, Quit game) — with no controller, that is how you
  reach it. The first time the controls appear on their own, a toast says so.
- **Per game:** *Game options › Controls* sets the layout (**Off** or **Gamepad**),
  whether the d-pad shows, where the right stick sits (**Off**, **Below A/B/X/Y** or
  **Above A/B/X/Y**) and the controls' opacity. *Settings › Controls* holds the
  defaults every game follows, and haptics.
- A controller connecting hides them and its disconnecting brings them back, so
  nothing is on screen twice; nothing stays held when a menu, a dialog or the app
  takes over.

### 🕹️ Any controller as a keyboard and mouse
Many games only listen to a keyboard and mouse, or want a different button than the
one your controller has. *Game options › Controls › Controller* (and *Settings ›
Controls › Controller* for every game's default) maps each input of a connected
controller — buttons, sticks, triggers, gyro — to **a controller input, a key with
modifiers, a mouse button, mouse movement or the wheel, or a Playport action**:

- **As a controller**, **As keyboard & mouse** or **Custom**, switched at the top of
  the page; Y puts a game's map back to the default. The page also opens over a
  paused game from the in-game menu.
- A stick bound to the mouse has its own **look speed** per game, so a shooter's aim
  is adjustable rather than all-or-nothing.

### 🎯 Gyro aim
Aim by moving the phone, or a DualSense, DualShock 4 or Switch Pro controller, with
the output as **the mouse or the right stick**, per game:

- *Game options › Controls › Gyro aim* has the source (**Auto**, so your controller's
  gyro when it has one, else the iPhone's), the output, sensitivity, an
  anti-dead-zone, smoothing, up/down ratio, the turn axis and inversions. A **test
  area** on the page follows the aim before you launch anything.
- *Settings › Controls › Gyro aim* holds the defaults; the in-game menu turns gyro aim
  on and off while you play, and a chip says when it starts.

### ⚡ Vulkan is every game's backend
Direct3D 9, 10, 11 and 12 now go to **Vulkan, on KosmicKrisp, Mesa's Vulkan driver on
Metal**, by default — the route taken by our Red Dead Redemption 2 work — with DXVK
and vkd3d-proton above it. DXMT stays one choice away in *Settings › Graphics ›
Direct3D* or on the game's page.

- **First launches are quicker:** KosmicKrisp compiles its shaders for Metal 4.0 on
  iOS 27, which the phone builds two to three times faster; a cold launch's shader
  compile dropped from about 17 s to about 14 s for Portal 2 and from about 7.5 s to
  about 5 s for Hollow Knight.
- **Second launches are smoother:** what the backends translate and compile is kept
  on disk (KosmicKrisp's and Zink's shaders, DXMT's), and so is the code FEX has
  translated, so a second launch and a second visit to an area no longer repeat the
  first one's work. *Settings › Storage › Shader cache* shows how much space they take
  and clears them, Metal's own caches included.

### 🖥️ OpenGL games run
Games with an OpenGL renderer now draw through Mesa's Zink on KosmicKrisp, at OpenGL
3.3, in 64-bit and 32-bit games alike: **Counter-Strike** and **Half-Life** reach a
map, **Neverwinter Nights: Enhanced Edition** gets into play, and other Windows
OpenGL games get their chance. All three are playable with issues listed on their
pages.

### 💀 Red Dead Redemption 2 plays
Red Dead Redemption 2's own Rockstar Games Launcher signs in, hands the game its
ticket, and is closed again — one click on Play, no launcher window to wrestle with,
and the game saves as it should after the launcher is gone.

**Playable with issues:** in our runs Story mode loads in about 80–95 s, the game
signs in about 20 s later, and one press on Story takes Arthur to the cabin, running
at roughly 16–19 fps with the footprint near the phone's limit (peaks of 7.2–7.7 of
8.2 GB). Some highlights are wrong (a white beard brim, a red ear) in bright light.
It installs about 120 GB; Story mode only, never Red Dead Online, in our testing.

### 🐺 The Witcher 3's 4.x build plays
The newer D3D12 build of **The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt** used to stop at once — it needs
AVX, which FEX on the phone did not offer. Playport's FEX now does, the game's 64 GiB
reservations are served, and it reaches Kaer Morhen and plays: about 24 fps at
1564×720 with hitches. The classic 1.32 build still plays at its 30 fps cap on DXMT.

### 🧠 More room for big games
Every Play's memory is planned from the phone's own memory and the game's need, and
memory the game frees is handed back for reuse instead of being lost:

- **The donor pool** keeps a large block of RAM outside Playport's own footprint and
  backs the game with it, so a title can use more than the app alone could hold.
  *Settings › Graphics › Donor memory* turns it **Off** or leaves it on **Auto**.
- **Per-phone defaults:** guest RAM, the video budget and the emulator's pool come
  from one table per phone, with the Vulkan clamp on by default.
- **Bigger titles fit:** in our testing Kingdom Come: Deliverance ran 32 minutes at a
  peak footprint of 3.9 GB where it used to reach 7.7 GB. A game that wants more than
  the phone can hold still ends: the pool widens the ceiling, it does not remove it.

### 🧊 Steam, better in 32-bit too
- **Playport's own Steam client now serves 32-bit games as well as 64-bit ones**, so a
  32-bit game gets the same sign-in, ownership check and online tickets a 64-bit one
  does instead of an emulated stand-in. Portal 2, which is 32-bit, reaches chapter 1
  this way.
- **Repair** fetches an app's shared depot again, so a game that lost Steamworks
  Shared files is repaired rather than reinstalled, and games sharing an install
  folder (Counter-Strike and Half-Life) join it file by file.
- A game Steam refuses ownership of is told apart from one Steam cannot check.

## Also new
- **Games get what they need, from Play:** a launch screen step (*Installing
  dependencies*) runs a title's own prerequisite installer once, downloads Steam's
  redistributables or GOG's dependencies where the store uses them, carries **Wine
  Mono** for .NET games and every Windows ANSI code page, and skips Steam's DirectX
  setup when nothing needs it.
- **Graphics choices are cleaner:** *Aspect ratio* and *Resolution* are separate
  choices, a game can have its own compatibility defaults (Super Meat Boy's 16:9, for
  one), and its page shows them in full under the player's own settings.
- **Games that change display mode** (GoldSrc's `-soft`, for instance) now present
  after the change instead of going blank.
- **Screenshot** now works over a Vulkan game, as does the **Performance overlay**.
- **Windows before the first frame:** a game's GDI windows and dialogs (a launcher's
  progress, an error box) show instead of an empty screen, and a game's own launcher
  gets the profile, Desktop and Start Menu it expects, with Windows services behind it.
- **Settings scrolls**, so every section fits a landscape phone; *Storage* lists
  Steamworks Shared with its size and Remove, the shader cache, and the emulator cache.
- **32-bit is first class:** DXVK's 32-bit Direct3D 10 and 11, the i386 .NET runtime,
  Steam's 32-bit client and the WoW64 registry paths.
- **Faster synchronisation:** Wine's in-process synchronisation now runs in its own
  thread-safe path, on by default, which is what Proton games expect.
- **Newer components:** Wine 11.20 with Valve's latest Proton Wine changes (and Wine
  Mono 11.3), FEX 5285020d with rpmalloc, current DXMT, DXVK, vkd3d-proton,
  Mesa/KosmicKrisp and the Khronos Vulkan loader.

## Good to know
- **Vulkan is the default now.** If a game looks or behaves worse than on 0.4.0,
  switch it to **DXMT** on its page (*Game options › Graphics › Direct3D*) and tell us
  which game: it is one setting, and it helps us pick the right default per title.
- **Red Dead Redemption 2 and The Witcher 3's 4.x build are memory-tight and slow:**
  16–19 fps and 24 fps respectively in the places we reached, with visual or hitch
  problems listed above. Reaching a place is not the same as playing it well.
- **Big games keep needing memory,** and a game that wants more than the phone can
  hold still ends.
- **No layout editor yet.** The on-screen controls are the Gamepad layout with its
  d-pad, right stick and opacity options; keyboard-and-mouse and point-and-click
  layouts and a movable layout are next.
- **Gyro aim drives the game's own stick or mouse**, so a game with its own aim
  settings still behaves as that game does.
- **Tested on one phone** (iOS 27.0.1). If a game misbehaves, *Report a problem* on
  its page shares one zip with its logs.
- Your games, saves and logins are untouched by this update; install over 0.4.0.

## Install

Download `Playport-0.5.0.ipa` and sideload it with your own Apple ID, keeping the
increased memory limit and the JIT helper extension. If you have an earlier version,
install over it. Bring your own games from Steam, GOG, Epic Games or Files.

The complete Corresponding Source of this exact build, the notices and the
rebuild instructions are attached as `Playport-0.5.0-source.tar`, `NOTICES.tar`
and `INSTALL-REBUILD.tar`. `SHA256SUMS` covers every file.

Playport is GPL-3.0-or-later; every component keeps its own licence.
