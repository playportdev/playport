# Hollow Knight gameplay at 720 rows: GPU load decides whether the game gets P cores

*The raw logs, tables and screenshots this record names were removed in the 2026-09-25 repository cleanup; they remain in git history.*

**Date:** 2026-09-25. **Tree:** the commit that adds `threads.txt` to
`harness/device/perf-run.py --analyze`. **Build:** the installed IPA of the
`vpad2` run in [the scripted-pad record](2026-09-25-hk-vpad-gameplay.md),
sha256 `fbdb311ec26ca8c715fb582efb691b435de28b5205f730df734a62f571693f70`
(no reinstall since). **Phone:** iPhone Air (`iPhone18,4`, A19 Pro: 2 P + 4 E
cores), iOS 27.0, over netmuxd Wi-Fi, on a 15 W charger; every run under
`flock $PLAYPORT_BUILD/device.lock`, each after 13 to 14 minutes idle.

**Result:** rendering the guest at 1564x720 (`HOST_SCREEN=720`) instead of the
panel's 2736x1260 keeps King's Pass gameplay at 120 FPS for about 100 s of play
and at 108 to 117 FPS for the rest of a 10-minute run. At full resolution the
same route runs at 38 to 66 FPS (`vpad2`, `vpad1`). At full resolution the GPU
takes the power budget and the scheduler moves the whole game onto E cores
at their lowest clocks. At 720 rows the game keeps the P cores. In the long
run the limit is then the P-core clock (1.31 GHz once the power budget is
2.35 W), and Unity's main thread is 94 % busy.

## 2. Where the CPU goes (million instructions per presented frame)

From the `threads.txt` tables. `Mi/f` does not change with the cluster or
clock the scheduler gave a thread, so it compares work across runs.

| thread | menu, native (`vpad2` t=45) | play, native (`vpad2` t=90) | play, 720 (`g720long` t=540) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0024` Unity main thread | 5.9 | 25.1 | 20.5 (94 % of a core, 82 % on P) |
| `00b0` UnityGfxDeviceWorker (D3D11 into DXMT) | 5.1 | 13.6 | 12.9 (53 %) |
| `00c0` unnamed: UnityPlayer.dll `+0x17d0e28`, the audio mixer | 3.1 | 19.7 | 7.1 (16 %) |
| `009c` dxmt-encode-thr | 2.6 | 6.2 | 6.0 (21 %) |
| all threads | 27 | 90 | 61 |

- The audio mixer's work is a fixed amount per second (about 780 to 850 M
  instructions/s in play). Its per-frame figure only grows as the frame rate
  falls.
- The main thread's work per frame is about the same at both resolutions
  (20 to 25 Mi/f). Resolution changes which cores it gets, not how much it
  does. At 120 FPS it needs about 2.5 G instructions/s. A P core at 3.7 GHz
  does that in 46 % of its time, but at 1.31 GHz it is saturated, so late in
  the run the frame rate follows the P clock.
- The census's `run=` field shows 3 to 4 threads runnable but waiting for a
  core at all times (for example `run=1213` ms in a 321 ms tick). Only two
  cores are P cores, and the main thread spends 18 % of its time on E cores.

## 3. What it means

- The fall to 40–55 FPS in gameplay is power, not CPU capacity: the GPU work
  of the full-resolution render takes the SoC's budget, and CLPC then gives the
  game no P-core time. A lower render resolution fixes most of it.
- The rest of the gap is CPU power. To hold 120 FPS at the budget the phone
  settles at (about 2.35 W), the per-frame work of the main thread, the
  graphics worker and DXMT's encoder must shrink, or run at a lower clock. The
  audio mixer's fixed cost and the wake-up traffic also need cutting (the
  `[sync-census]` lines show 5,000 to 8,000 thread alerts/s and 1,000 to
  1,400 server requests/s).
- Frame-time spikes of 20.8 to 62 ms remain in steady play (the `ftmax`
  column), on top of the 100 to 240 ms hitches when a scene loads.
